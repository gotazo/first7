## Meaning

A rod is a straight, slender stick or staff. It may be used for support, as a tool, for discipline, or as a symbol of authority.

## Biblical Usage

When the LORD asked Moses what he held in his hand, Moses answered, “A rod” (Exodus 4:2). The LORD instructed him to take the rod and use it in performing signs (Exodus 4:17).

Moses' rod became a serpent when cast to the ground and returned to a rod when Moses took it by the tail (Exodus 4:3-4). It was also used in signs before Pharaoh and in events during Israel's journey, including the dividing of the sea and the provision of water from the rock (Exodus 14:16; 17:5-6).

Aaron also had a rod associated with signs in Egypt, and his rod later budded as recorded in Numbers 17.

The English word *rod* represents different original-language words across Scripture. The Hebrew word *matteh* used in Exodus 4:2 can also mean a branch or a tribe in other contexts.

## Key References

- **Exodus 4:2** — Moses identifies the object in his hand as a rod.
- **Exodus 4:17** — The rod is to be used in performing signs.
- **Exodus 7:9-12** — Aaron casts down his rod before Pharaoh.
- **Exodus 14:16** — Moses is instructed to lift up his rod over the sea.
- **Exodus 17:5-6** — Moses is instructed to take the rod and strike the rock.
- **Numbers 17:8** — Aaron's rod buds, blossoms, and produces almonds.
- **Psalm 23:4** — David speaks of the LORD's rod and staff.

## Related Terms

- **Staff** — A stick carried or used for support.
- **Sceptre** — A staff or rod representing royal authority.
- **Moses** — The man who carried the rod used in the signs described in Exodus.
- **Aaron** — Moses' brother, whose rod is also mentioned in Exodus and Numbers.
- **Serpent** — The creature into which Moses' rod was changed as a sign.
