## Meaning

Lamentation is an expression of deep sorrow, grief, or regret, often in words or a formal composition.

---

## Biblical Usage

Scripture uses *lamentation* for expressions of sorrow over death, disaster, judgment, and destruction. A lamentation may take the form of spoken words, a song, or a composed declaration of grief.

In 2 Samuel 1:17-27, David laments Saul and Jonathan, and the passage records the lamentation he composed concerning them. Other biblical passages use lamentations concerning rulers, nations, and cities.

---

## Key References

- **2 Samuel 1:17-27** — Records David's lamentation for Saul and Jonathan.
- **2 Chronicles 35:25** — Describes lamentations made for Josiah.
- **Jeremiah 7:29** — Calls for a lamentation in connection with judgment.
- **Ezekiel 19:1** — Introduces a lamentation for the princes of Israel.
- **Ezekiel 27:2** — Commands a lamentation for Tyrus.
- **Ezekiel 32:2** — Introduces a lamentation concerning Pharaoh.
- **Amos 5:1** — Introduces a lamentation over the house of Israel.
- **Matthew 2:18** — Uses language of lamentation and weeping in the quotation concerning Rachel.

## Related Terms

- **Mourning** — The expression of grief, especially after a death.
- **Lament** — An expression of sorrow or grief.
- **Grief** — Deep sorrow, especially following loss.
- **Weeping** — The shedding of tears as an expression of emotion.
