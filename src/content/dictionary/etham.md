## Meaning

Etham is a place-name mentioned in the biblical account of the Israelites' journey from Egypt. Some reference works propose a connection with the Egyptian word *khetam*, meaning “fortress” or “castle,” but this derivation is uncertain.

## Biblical Usage

After leaving Succoth, the Israelites camped at Etham, described as being at the edge of the wilderness (Exodus 13:20). Numbers also records Etham as a stopping place on their journey (Numbers 33:6-8).

The precise location of Etham is uncertain. The proposed connection between its name and the Egyptian word *khetam* is disputed and should not be treated as an established meaning.

## Key References

- **Exodus 13:20** — The Israelites camped at Etham at the edge of the wilderness.
- **Numbers 33:6** — Etham is recorded as a stopping place after Succoth.
- **Numbers 33:7-8** — The journey continues toward the area of Pi-hahiroth and the sea.

## Related Terms

- **Succoth** — A place where the Israelites camped before Etham.
- **Wilderness** — A sparsely inhabited or uncultivated region.
- **Pi-hahiroth** — A place mentioned in the account of Israel's departure from Egypt.
- **Marah** — A place reached during the wilderness journey.
