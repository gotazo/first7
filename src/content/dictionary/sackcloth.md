## Meaning

Sackcloth is a coarse cloth used in biblical times as a garment or covering. It is often associated with mourning, grief, repentance, or distress.

---

## Biblical Usage

Scripture describes people wearing sackcloth during periods of grief, calamity, repentance, or distress. It is often mentioned with ashes, fasting, and weeping as an outward expression of a person's circumstances or response.

In 2 Samuel 3:31, David commands Joab and the people to gird themselves with sackcloth and mourn for Abner. In Esther 4:1, Mordecai puts on sackcloth and ashes after learning of the decree against the Jews. Other passages describe its use in repentance and prophetic activity.

---

## Key References

- **Genesis 37:34** — Jacob puts sackcloth upon his loins while mourning Joseph.
- **2 Samuel 3:31** — David commands the people to wear sackcloth and mourn for Abner.
- **1 Kings 21:27** — Ahab puts on sackcloth in response to a declaration of judgment.
- **Esther 4:1-3** — Mordecai and others wear sackcloth in response to the decree against the Jews.
- **Psalm 35:13** — David describes humbling himself with sackcloth.
- **Jonah 3:5-8** — The people of Nineveh, including the king, respond with fasting and sackcloth.
- **Matthew 11:21** — Jesus refers to repentance in sackcloth and ashes.
- **Revelation 11:3** — The two witnesses are described as prophesying clothed in sackcloth.

---

## Related Terms

- **Mourning** — The expression of grief, especially after a death.
- **Ashes** — The powdery remains of burned material, also used in biblical expressions of grief or humility.
- **Fasting** — Abstaining from food for a period, often in connection with prayer or mourning.
- **Repentance** — Turning from wrongdoing or changing one's mind and conduct.
