truth: The influences believers allow into their lives can affect their thinking and conduct, so they must choose them wisely.

insight:
  - Paul warns that evil communications corrupt good manners, showing that the company a person keeps can affect conduct.
  - Repeated exposure to certain ways of thinking and behaving can make them seem normal.
  - Becoming a believer does not mean that every former habit or pattern of thinking disappears immediately.
  - Spiritual growth includes learning to recognize influences that do not agree with God's Word.
  - The answer is not merely to identify harmful influences but also to give attention to what is true, good, and pleasing to God.
  - The mind and conduct of a believer should increasingly reflect the truth of Scripture rather than the patterns of the surrounding world.

application:
  - Examine how your friendships and regular conversations influence your thinking and conduct.
  - Be careful about repeatedly listening to teaching that contradicts God's Word.
  - Choose fellowship that encourages faithfulness to Christ.
  - Notice habits of thought or conduct that you have adopted from harmful influences.
  - Fill your mind with what is true and good according to Scripture.
  - Allow God's Word to correct your thinking and shape your daily conduct.
