truth: God's mercy welcomes the repentant sinner, and His acceptance is not earned by trying to prove one's worth through works.

insight:
  - The younger son acknowledged his sin and returned to his father rather than remaining in his former condition.
  - The father responded with compassion and welcomed his returning son instead of leaving him to his shame.
  - The father's response shows that mercy, not the son's ability to repay his losses, shaped the welcome he received.
  - The elder son's reaction reveals the danger of measuring a relationship with the father by years of service and expected rewards.
  - The father reminded the elder son that he already belonged in the household and that what the father had was available to him.
  - God's mercy should lead to humility and gratitude rather than comparison, resentment, or an attempt to earn what is given through grace.

application:
  - Return to God honestly when you recognize your sin rather than hiding from Him in shame.
  - Acknowledge wrongdoing and respond through repentance instead of making excuses.
  - Receive God's mercy with gratitude rather than trying to establish your worth through religious activity.
  - Do not measure God's goodness toward you by comparing your life with someone else's.
  - Rejoice when others turn back to God and receive His mercy.
  - Let gratitude for God's grace lead you into faithful obedience rather than an attempt to earn His love.
