# First7 Teaching Standard

## Purpose

Every First7 teaching exists to help people understand what Scripture teaches through a concise, faithful, and practical Bible study.

Teachings are **not**:
- Sermon transcripts
- Commentaries
- Opinion articles
- Devotionals

Scripture is the final authority.

---

# First7 Editorial Principle

> **Scripture is the foundation.**
>
> **The sermon is the guide.**
>
> **Truth is the destination.**

Every published teaching must be:

- Faithful to Scripture.
- Informed by a faithful sermon.
- Free from speculation.
- Clear enough for new believers.
- Rich enough for lifelong study.

## Editorial Principle

Insights should capture the **biblical principle being developed**, not merely retell the biblical event.

The purpose of an insight is to help the reader understand what the passage teaches, rather than simply recount what happened.

### Weak

- David defeated Goliath.
- Jesus calmed the storm.
- The four lepers went to the Syrian camp.

### Strong

- Faith trusts God rather than outward circumstances.
- Christ's presence brings peace in the midst of life's storms.
- The four lepers refused to remain where the outcome was certain, choosing instead to move forward and entrust the outcome to God.

Each insight should answer:

**"What biblical principle is being developed here?"**

not merely:

**"What happened in the passage?"**

# Choosing the Teaching

Every teaching should have:

- One primary truth.
- One primary Scripture.
- One clear purpose.
- One distinct application.

If a sermon develops several distinct truths, create separate teachings.

Do not create additional teachings merely to increase the number extracted from a sermon.

Stop when the sermon has been faithfully captured.

**Quality is more important than quantity.**

---

# Choosing the Primary Scripture

The primary Scripture should:

- Directly support the Truth.
- Be the passage most developed in the sermon.
- Become the foundation for the insights.

Supporting Scriptures strengthen the Truth but never replace the primary passage.

---

# Scripture Standards

## Primary Scripture

- Full KJV quotation.
- Never abbreviate.
- Never paraphrase.

## Supporting Scriptures

- References only.
- Do not include verse text.

---

# Truth Standard

The Truth is the central biblical teaching of the First7 teaching.

Rules:

- One sentence.
- Directly supported by the primary Scripture.
- Clear.
- Memorable.
- Every insight must support the Truth.

The Truth should express the central biblical principle rather than simply summarize the sermon.

---

# Insight Standard

Insights are the heart of every First7 teaching.

Insights are extracted from the sermon and verified by Scripture.

Every insight must satisfy **both** conditions:

1. It is clearly developed in the sermon.
2. It is supported by Scripture.

The sermon determines **what we extract**.

Scripture determines **what we publish**.

## Published Insights

The editorial process must never appear in the published teaching.

Never write:

- "The sermon teaches..."
- "The preacher said..."
- "According to the message..."
- "This sermon explains..."

Write every insight as a concise biblical truth.

The reader should encounter the biblical principle directly, without being told where the insight came from.

## Do

- Explain the biblical principle being developed.
- Extract the preacher's developed biblical observations.
- Rewrite them in First7 language.
- Keep them concise.
- Keep them evergreen.
- Preserve the distinctive point being developed.
- Verify them with Scripture.

## Do Not

- Invent insights.
- Add personal opinions.
- Add unrelated biblical ideas.
- Speculate.
- Expand beyond what was developed.
- Turn applications into insights.
- Retell the biblical event without explaining its principle.
- Generalize a distinctive sermon point until its original meaning is lost.
- Add insights merely to increase the number of insights.

---

# Preserve The Sermon's Distinctive Point

When a sermon develops a specific biblical scene, contrast, connection, or emphasis, preserve that distinctiveness in the insight.

Do not replace a specific sermon-developed principle with a broader or more generic statement.

The goal is not to make every insight sound universally applicable by removing the details that make the biblical point meaningful.

### Example

Weak:

- Do not judge people by outward appearance.

Strong:

- The woman who came to Jesus was known by the religious leaders according to what they believed about her, and they questioned Jesus because they saw her as a sinner who should not be touching Him.
- Jesus did not respond to the woman merely according to the reputation the religious leaders attached to her.
- The contrast is striking: the priests saw a prostitute, while Jesus saw Israel.

The stronger version preserves the specific biblical picture and the distinctive point being developed rather than reducing it to a general statement.

### Editorial Rule

**Do not merely retell the event.**

**Do not generalize the sermon into something generic.**

**Capture the specific biblical principle being developed while preserving the distinctive point that gives the insight its meaning.**

# Insight Checklist

Before accepting an insight, ask:

- Was this clearly developed in the sermon?
- Is it supported by Scripture?
- Does it explain the biblical principle?
- Does it support the Truth?
- Can it stand alone?
- Does it preserve the distinctive point?
- Is it necessary?
- Does it add something different from the other insights?

If an insight fails these questions, rewrite it or remove it.

---

# Application Standard

Applications answer:

**How should the believer respond?**

They should be:

- Practical.
- Biblical.
- Simple.
- Actionable.
- Connected to the Truth and insights.

Applications should not introduce new doctrines or ideas that were not established in the teaching.

---

# Writing Style

Every teaching should have:

- One central truth.
- One clear topic.
- Simple language.
- Concise insights.
- Practical applications.
- Evergreen value.

Avoid unnecessary theological jargon.

Write so that a new believer can understand the teaching while still giving enough substance for mature believers.

---

# Avoid Repetition

Before creating a teaching, ask:

- Does this Truth already exist?
- Does another First7 teaching already cover the same principle?
- Are the insights genuinely different?
- Does this teaching contribute something new?
- Could two proposed teachings be merged without losing an important distinction?

If two teachings substantially overlap, merge them.

Do not create multiple teachings from the same sermon point merely by changing the title.

---

# Choosing Topics

The topic should reflect the **actual central subject** of the teaching.

Do not automatically place a teaching under `Faith` simply because the teaching involves trusting God.

Choose the most accurate category.

For example:

- Dependence upon God → `dependence-on-god`
- God's direction → `gods-will`
- Assurance of God's presence → `assurance`
- Christian perseverance → `perseverance`
- God's purpose → `purpose`
- Teachings about Christ Himself → `christ`
- Teachings about the person and work of Christ as a theological subject → `christology`
- Spiritual discernment → `spiritual-discernment`

The category should follow the teaching's central emphasis.

# Cross References

## Terms

List important biblical words introduced or developed in the teaching.

## Related

List other First7 teachings that naturally connect with the teaching.

Related teachings should actually exist in the project.

## Keywords

Use concise, meaningful, search-friendly phrases.

Do not add keywords merely for search volume.

# KJV Standard

All Scripture quotations use the **King James Version (KJV)**.

- Never modernize Scripture.
- Never paraphrase Scripture.
- Never alter KJV wording.
- Always preserve the complete quotation for the primary Scripture.

---

# The First7 Test

Hide the Scripture.

Read only:

- Truth
- Insights
- Application

Ask:

**Is the biblical principle immediately clear?**

If the answer is no, rewrite the teaching.

The teaching should communicate its central truth even when the reader is temporarily looking only at the Truth, Insights, and Application.

---

# Editorial Checklist

Before publishing, ask:

- Is the Truth directly supported by the primary Scripture?
- Is the primary Scripture the right foundation for the teaching?
- Are the insights clearly developed in the sermon?
- Are the insights supported by Scripture?
- Does each insight explain a biblical principle?
- Does each insight preserve the distinctive point when necessary?
- Is there any speculation?
- Is anything added that was not developed?
- Are there unnecessary or repetitive insights?
- Is the application practical and biblical?
- Is the KJV used?
- Is the teaching evergreen?
- Is the teaching distinct from existing teachings?
- Is the topic appropriate?
- Does it follow the schema exactly?

# Summary

Every First7 teaching follows this principle:

**Truth comes from Scripture.**

**Insights are extracted from the sermon and verified by Scripture.**

**Applications are faithful responses to God's Word.**

The sermon is used to identify and develop the biblical truths worth teaching.

Scripture remains the final authority.

The goal is not to extract the maximum number of teachings from a sermon.

The goal is to faithfully capture the **best biblical truths actually developed**, write them clearly, and preserve their distinctive meaning.

**Quality over quantity.**

**Faithfulness over creativity.**

**Biblical truth over speculation.**
