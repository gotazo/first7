# First7 Bible Dictionary Style Guide

## Purpose

The First7 Bible Dictionary exists to help readers understand biblical
words, people, places, nations, objects, events, and concepts using a
Scripture-first, neutral, and easy-to-read approach.

It is not intended to be a commentary, devotional, or theological
reference.

The goal is to define each term clearly, explain how the Bible uses it,
and provide key Scripture references for further study.

---

# Mission

To provide clear, neutral, Scripture-first definitions of biblical
words, people, places, nations, objects, events, and concepts that help
readers understand what Scripture says without adding interpretation.

Every entry is designed to:

- Define the biblical term clearly.
- Describe how the term is used in Scripture.
- Connect readers to related biblical subjects.
- Encourage further study through representative Scripture references.

---

# Vision

To become one of the most trusted free Scripture-first Bible
dictionaries, helping readers explore the Bible through clear
definitions, meaningful cross-references, and an interconnected study
experience.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

Core Principles

Every dictionary entry should:

-   Be concise.
-   Be factual.
-   Be easy to scan.
-   Use simple language.
-   Follow the same structure.
-   Let Scripture speak for itself.

Avoid:

-   Personal opinions.
-   Devotional writing.
-   Sermons.
-   Speculation.
-   Denominational viewpoints.
-   Interpreting prophecy.
-   Historical traditions that are not clearly found in Scripture.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Editorial Philosophy

The First7 Bible Dictionary is a Scripture-first reference work.

Its purpose is to define biblical terms clearly and describe how they
are used in Scripture without interpreting them.

Every dictionary entry should answer two questions:

1.  What does this term mean?
2.  How is this term used in Scripture?

The Meaning section provides a concise dictionary definition.

The Biblical Usage section explains how the Bible uses the term.

The dictionary describes what Scripture says. It does not explain what
readers should believe about the term or how it should be interpreted.

Whenever a subject requires theological explanation, doctrinal
discussion, devotional application, or prophetic interpretation, those
topics belong in the Teachings or Bible Prophecy sections rather than in
the dictionary.

------------------------------------------------------------------------


## Scope of the Dictionary

The First7 Bible Dictionary is a reference work that defines biblical
terms.

Its purpose is to explain what a word or name means and describe how it
is used in Scripture.

The dictionary is not intended to provide complete biographies of
biblical people, detailed histories of places, or comprehensive studies
of biblical events.

When a person, place, or event requires more detailed study, the
dictionary should provide a concise description and direct readers to
the appropriate First7 study resources.

The dictionary should always answer two questions:

1. What does this term mean?
2. How is this term used in Scripture?

---

## Bible Translation

The King James Version (KJV) is the only Bible translation used in
First7.

Rules:

-   Quote only the KJV.
-   Never paraphrase Scripture.
-   Preserve the exact wording of Scripture quotations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dictionary Structure

Every entry follows the same structure.

1. Meaning

The Meaning section provides a concise dictionary definition appropriate
to the type of entry.

Choose the style of definition based on the category of the term.

Common English Words

For ordinary English words, give the standard dictionary definition
before discussing the Bible.

Examples:

Offering

  Something that is presented, given, or offered to another person or
  for a particular purpose.

Peace

  A state of calm, harmony, or freedom from conflict.

Authority

  The power or right to command, govern, or make decisions.

Do not explain biblical usage here.
---

## Multiple People Sharing the Same Name

Many biblical names refer to more than one person.

In the First7 Bible Dictionary, create a single dictionary entry for the
name itself rather than separate entries for each individual.

The **Meaning** section should define the name.

The **Biblical Usage** section should briefly identify the different
people who bear that name and provide representative Scripture
references.

Example:

**Enoch**

- Enoch, the son of Cain (Genesis 4:17)
- Enoch, the descendant of Seth (Genesis 5:18–24)

**James**

- James, the son of Zebedee
- James, the son of Alphaeus
- James, the brother of Jesus

**John**

- John the Baptist
- John the Apostle
- John Mark

This helps readers understand that the same name may refer to different
individuals in Scripture while keeping the dictionary focused on the
term itself.

## Biblical People, Places, Nations, and Events

For biblical people, places, nations, and events, identify what the term is before describing its biblical usage.

Keep the definition factual, concise, and neutral.

Examples:

**Anakim**

> A people mentioned in the Old Testament who lived in the land of Canaan.

**Bashan**

> A region east of the Jordan River.

**Armageddon**

> The name of a place mentioned in the Book of Revelation.

Do not expand into biblical explanation in the Meaning section.

---

## Proper Names

For biblical proper names, the **Meaning** section should define the name itself before describing the biblical person, place, nation, or event.

When the original Hebrew, Aramaic, or Greek meaning of a proper name is clear or widely accepted and reasonably established, it should normally be reflected in the **Meaning** section.

Examples:

**Adam**

> A Hebrew proper name meaning "man," "mankind," or "humanity."

**Eve**

> A Hebrew proper name meaning "life" or "living."

**Abraham**

> A Hebrew proper name meaning "father of a multitude."

**Bethlehem**

> A Hebrew place name meaning "house of bread."

**Jerusalem**

> A Hebrew place name traditionally understood as "foundation of peace" or "city of peace."

The **Biblical Usage** section should then identify who or what the term refers to in Scripture and describe how the Bible uses it.

For example:

**Meaning**

> Adam is a Hebrew proper name meaning "man," "mankind," or "humanity."

**Biblical Usage**

> In Scripture, Adam is the name of the first man created by God.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Name Meanings

When Scripture explicitly explains the meaning of a biblical name,
prefer the biblical explanation.

When the meaning of a name is widely accepted from Hebrew, Aramaic, or
Greek, present it concisely.

When the meaning is uncertain or debated, state that it is uncertain or
traditionally understood, rather than presenting a speculative meaning
as fact.

Avoid listing multiple scholarly theories unless they are necessary for
understanding the biblical term.

--------------------------------------------------

Biblical Offices, Beings, and Titles

For offices, beings, and titles, define the role.

Examples:

Angel

  A spiritual being who serves as a messenger or servant of God.

Apostle

  A person sent with a mission or commission.

Prophet

  A person who speaks a message on behalf of God.

Priest

  A person appointed to perform sacred duties and minister before God.

Do not expand into biblical explanation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Uniquely Biblical Terms

Some words exist primarily as biblical terms rather than ordinary
English words.

Examples:

-   Sheol
-   Hades
-   Gehenna
-   Tartarus
-   Tabernacle

Define these according to their biblical identity while remaining
descriptive rather than interpretive.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. Biblical Usage

After defining the term, explain how it is used in Scripture.

This section should:

-   Describe biblical usage.
-   Remain neutral.
-   Avoid interpretation.
-   Avoid doctrinal conclusions.
-   Let Scripture explain the subject.

Good example:

  In Scripture, an offering is something presented to God. Offerings
  included animals, grain, oil, drink offerings, incense, and other
  gifts brought according to God’s instructions.

Poor example:

  Offerings represent Christ’s sacrifice and demonstrate our complete
  surrender.

The second example is interpretation rather than dictionary description.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. Key References

List the principal passages where the word appears or is clearly
illustrated.

Do not attempt to list every occurrence.

Choose passages that best define the biblical usage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

4. Related Terms

Connect readers naturally to other dictionary entries.

Related terms should represent meaningful biblical relationships.

Examples:

-   Sacrifice
-   Altar
-   Priest
-   Offering

The goal is to build an interconnected Bible dictionary where readers
can naturally continue studying related subjects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Writing Style

Use:

-   Short sentences.
-   Plain English.
-   Neutral tone.
-   Descriptive language.
-   Consistent formatting.

Avoid:

-   Long explanations.
-   Repetition.
-   Commentary.
-   Application.
-   Speculation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

People

For biblical people:

-   Identify who the person is.
-   State why the person appears in Scripture.
-   Keep the information factual.
-   Avoid writing biographies.

Example:

  Og was the king of Bashan during the time of Moses.

Not:

  Og represents spiritual opposition against God’s people.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Places

For places:

Explain:

-   What the place is.
-   Where it appears in Scripture.
-   Why it is significant in the biblical record.

Do not include unnecessary historical traditions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nations and People Groups

Examples:

-   Anakim
-   Rephaim
-   Philistines
-   Amalekites

Provide:

-   Plain identification.
-   Biblical references.
-   Neutral description.

Do not speculate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Objects

Examples:

-   Lamp
-   Altar
-   Ark
-   Scroll
-   Incense

Begin with the ordinary dictionary definition.

Then explain how Scripture uses the object.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Events

Examples:

-   Flood
-   Exodus
-   Resurrection

Identify the event first.

Then describe its biblical usage.

Do not expand into a Bible study.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Prophecy

Prophecy requires special care.

Never interpret prophetic passages.

Do not explain future events beyond what Scripture plainly states.

Instead:

-   Define the term.
-   Describe how it is used.
-   List the principal passages.

Examples:

-   Beast
-   Horn
-   Mark
-   Dragon
-   Day of the Lord

Remain descriptive rather than interpretive.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hebrew and Greek

Include the original language only when appropriate.

Provide:

-   Original word.
-   Transliteration.
-   Brief lexical meaning.

Do not provide extended word studies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Scripture References

Choose representative passages.

Quality is more important than quantity.

Prefer passages that define or clearly demonstrate the biblical usage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Related vs See Also

Related

Related terms are closely connected biblical concepts.

These may already exist in the dictionary or be planned future entries.

The purpose is to show the natural relationship between biblical
subjects.

Example:

    related:
      - sacrifice
      - altar
      - priest

------------------------------------------------------------------------

See Also

See Also points readers to broader or supplementary topics that
naturally expand the study.

Example:

    seeAlso:
      - burnt offering
      - meat offering
      - peace offering

As the dictionary grows, many See Also terms may later become Related
Terms.

Common English Words

One Concept = One Entry

Each dictionary entry should define one distinct biblical concept.

Do not combine multiple concepts into a single entry simply because they
share a similar name.

Examples:

  Separate Entries       Reason
  ---------------------- -----------------------------------------
  Firstfruits            The first portion of a harvest.
  Feast of Firstfruits   An appointed feast.
  Trumpet                A musical instrument.
  Feast of Trumpets      An appointed feast.
  Altar                  A structure for offerings.
  Brazen Altar           The bronze altar in the court.
  Golden Altar           The altar of incense in the Holy Place.
  Priest                 A priestly office.
  High Priest            The chief priest.
  Jerusalem              The historical city.
  New Jerusalem          The city described in Revelation.

Closely related entries should be connected through Related Terms and
See Also, while each page remains focused on a single biblical concept.

## General Before Specific

Whenever both a general concept and a specific biblical instance deserve
their own dictionary entries, create separate entries for each.

Write the broader concept first, then create separate entries for the
specific biblical examples.

This keeps each entry focused on one subject while allowing readers to
move naturally from general concepts to specific biblical terms.

Examples:

General                Specific
---------------------- ----------------------------------------------
Tree                   Tree of Life
Tree                   Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil

Tower                  Tower of Babel

Feast                  Feast of Passover
Feast                  Feast of Trumpets
Feast                  Feast of Tabernacles

Offering               Burnt Offering
Offering               Peace Offering
Offering               Sin Offering

Altar                  Brazen Altar
Altar                  Golden Altar

Priest                 High Priest

Place                  Garden of Eden
Region                 Eden

When possible, readers should be able to begin with the general concept
and continue to the more specific biblical entries through Related Terms
and See Also.

This principle helps build an interconnected Bible dictionary while
keeping every page focused on a single biblical concept.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

Canonical Entry Names

Each concept should have one canonical dictionary entry.

Alternative names should normally be listed as aliases rather than
creating duplicate entries.

Examples:

Canonical:

-   Most Holy Place

Aliases:

-   Holy of Holies
-   Holiest of All

Canonical:

-   Meat Offering

Alias:

-   Grain Offering

Canonical:

-   Ark of the Covenant

Alias:

-   Ark

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cross-Linking

Every entry should contribute to an interconnected Bible dictionary.

When appropriate:

-   Link to broader concepts.
-   Link to narrower concepts.
-   Link to related people.
-   Link to related places.
-   Link to related objects.
-   Link to related events.

Example:

Tabernacle

Related:

-   Holy Place
-   Most Holy Place
-   Ark of the Covenant
-   Priest
-   Laver

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Entry Scope

Each entry should focus on one clearly identifiable biblical subject.

Do not attempt to explain every related topic on the same page.

If another subject deserves its own definition, create a separate entry.

The dictionary should grow through interconnected pages rather than
longer articles.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When Not to Create a Dictionary Entry

Do not create entries for:

-   Common English words that add little value to Bible study.
-   Pure grammatical words.
-   Modern theological terminology not found in Scripture.
-   Denominational terminology.
-   Duplicate concepts already covered elsewhere.

Every entry should help readers understand Scripture more clearly.

Entry Length

Aim for concise entries.

Meaning:

1–2 sentences

Biblical Usage:

2–4 short paragraphs

Key References:

Representative passages

The dictionary should remain quick to read and easy to scan.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Growing the Dictionary

As the dictionary expands, new entries should strengthen existing areas
rather than simply increasing the total number of pages.

Priority should be given to:

-   Completing related collections.
-   Improving cross-links.
-   Filling gaps in biblical coverage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Source Priority

When writing dictionary entries, use sources in the following order:

1.  Scripture
2.  Biblical languages (Hebrew and Greek)
3.  Plain English dictionary definitions
4.  Historical and geographical facts that directly explain Scripture

Do not allow secondary sources to override the biblical text.

Where historical or linguistic information is included, it should help
readers understand Scripture rather than replace it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consistency

Maintain consistent wording throughout the dictionary.

Examples:

Use:

-   “In Scripture…”
-   “According to God’s commandments…”
-   “The Bible describes…”

Avoid unnecessary variation simply for stylistic purposes.

Consistency is preferred over creativity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Entry Independence

Each dictionary entry should be understandable on its own.

Readers should not need to open another entry to understand the basic
definition.

Related Terms expand study but should not be required to understand the
current entry.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition Before Description

Always define the term before describing its biblical usage.

Meaning answers:

“What is it?”

Biblical Usage answers:

“How is it used in Scripture?”

Never combine these into a single explanation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

-   title

-   offering

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Guiding Principle

The First7 Bible Dictionary exists to provide clear, concise, and
Scripture-first explanations that help readers understand biblical terms
without interpreting Scripture for them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Final Checklist

Before publishing an entry, confirm:

-   The Meaning is a proper dictionary definition.
-   The Biblical Usage is descriptive and Scripture-first.
-   The writing is concise and neutral.
-   Scripture quotations are KJV.
-   No interpretation has been added.
-   Key references are representative.
-   Hebrew and Greek information is concise.
-   Related terms are meaningful.
-   Formatting matches the First7 template.
-   The entry is consistent with the First7 editorial philosophy.

If every item is satisfied, the entry is ready for publication.
